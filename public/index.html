Transparent by construction

Every assumption.
Every cash flow.

A free cash flow to the firm model, with explicit reinvestment, actual-date discounting and a separate equity bridge. Inspect the calculation and its limits.

01

Build an explicit operating forecast

Revenue grows in three periods: 2027–2030, 2031–2033 and 2034–2036. EBIT margins interpolate through the 2027, 2030 and 2036 assumptions. The 2026 revenue input is a model starting point and is identified separately from disclosed revenue or run-rate.

Revenuet = Revenuet−1 × (1 + growtht)
02

Charge growth for the capital it requires

Positive EBIT is taxed at the assumed rate. Net reinvestment equals incremental revenue divided by sales-to-invested-capital. That amount includes net capex and working capital; adding either separately would count it twice.

FCFF = EBIT − tax − net reinvestment

The model does not include NOL carryforwards or tax assets. Negative EBIT receives no modeled tax benefit.

03

Discount from the actual valuation date

The remaining-2026 FCFF input is discounted from 18 October 2026 to year-end. Full forecast cash flows are discounted to each subsequent year-end using elapsed days divided by 365.25. The stub appears once in enterprise value.

PV(FCFFt) = FCFFt / (1 + WACC)elapsed days / 365.25
04

Make terminal reinvestment explicit

Terminal cash flow uses next-year NOPAT and reinvestment consistent with perpetual growth and terminal return on invested capital. WACC and terminal ROIC must both exceed perpetual growth. The model exposes how much EV comes from terminal value and how terminal FCFF changes from 2036.

Terminal FCFF = NOPAT2037 × (1 − g / terminal ROIC)
Terminal value = Terminal FCFF / (WACC − g)
05

Keep enterprise and equity value distinct

The primary result values the operating business. The optional bridge adds assumed cash and subtracts assumed debt and other claims. Completed funding-round prices, reported asks and capital commitments retain their own labels; none is silently substituted for enterprise value.

Read the limits with the result

  • All forecasts are illustrative assumptions, not management guidance unless explicitly attributed.
  • Peak cumulative FCFF deficit is a simplified operating funding indicator. Opening liquidity, intrayear timing and contractual payments are unknown.
  • Terminal cash flow can differ sharply from the last explicit year because reinvestment changes basis.
  • A revenue accounting change requires corresponding adjustments to margins and capital efficiency; rescaling revenue alone changes the economics.
  • The reverse DCF holds every assumption except early growth fixed and does not estimate a probability.
