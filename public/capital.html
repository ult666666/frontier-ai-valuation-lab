How the ranking works

In each scenario, the benchmark is the highest value to existing shareholders among plans that meet both objectives. A plan’s shortfall is the benchmark minus its value. The ranking returns the plans with the smallest worst shortfall across scenarios (minimax regret), including ties. It never changes your selection.

Break-even equity values

The equity value before the round at which two plans give existing shareholders the same value.

Valuation range supported by every scenario

The ownership floor sets a minimum pre-money valuation for each plan. The model sets a maximum: the valuation that every scenario’s equity value covers at the ratio you choose. When the minimum is above the maximum, no valuation meets both.

At 1×, each scenario’s equity value must at least equal the pre-money valuation.

    Method and limits

    Each plan is valued with the same operating case. The equity bridge turns operating enterprise value (EV) into equity value before the round, and the capital raised is then added as cash.

    Existing shareholders’ value = ownership retained × (equity value before the round + capital raised)

    • Round valuations, balances and objectives are inputs. The model does not estimate what investors would pay or how much capital a company needs.
    • There are no fees, preferences, option pools, convertibles or secondary sales, and operations do not change with the new capital.
    • Equity values can be negative. They are model outputs, not share prices.
    • Meeting the objectives does not mean a round is available. A finite continuation counts no cash flow or residual value after its cutoff.
